FANTASY GENRE

The fantasy genre in manhwa (South Korean comics) refers to stories built around magic, supernatural phenomena, mythic worlds, or unreal settings that do not exist in the real world. Because manhwa are heavily driven by digital webtoon platforms, the fantasy genre has evolved into several massive, distinct subgenres rather than just classic medieval magic.


Core Subgenres of Fantasy Manhwa

Gate / Hunter / System Fantasy:

Modern-day Earth is invaded by portals (gates) filled with monsters, and regular people awaken video-game-like abilities. Protagonists often have a floating "stat window," skill trees, or leveling systems (e.g., Solo Leveling, Omniscient Reader’s Viewpoint).

Isekai / Reincarnation / Transmigration:

A modern person dies or wakes up inside the body of a character in a novel, game, or historical romance story. A very popular subset is the "villainess" genre, where the protagonist reincarnates as the doomed antagonist of a romance-fantasy novel and tries to change her fate (e.g., Survive As The Hero’s Wife).

Historical & Medieval Fantasy:

Set in fictional European-style kingdoms with dukes, emperors, magic towers, and royal politics—frequently paired with romance (e.g., The Remarried Empress, Duke Pendragon).

Urban & Modern Fantasy:

Stories set in contemporary South Korea (like Seoul) where hidden supernatural beings, grim reapers, demon hunters, or gods live secretly among humans (e.g., The Uncanny Counter).

High & Epic Fantasy:

Traditional worldbuilding featuring expansive magic systems, non-human races (elves, dragons, demons), and long-running quests (e.g., Tower of God).

ACTION GENRE

The action genre in manhwa (South Korean digital comics/webtoons) refers to stories centered around high-stakes physical conflicts, dynamic fight choreography, and fast-paced sequences. Unlike traditional print manga, action manhwa are specifically formatted for vertical scrolling, which creators use to build tension, stage sweeping battle panels, and deliver high-impact cinematic moments.

Core Subgenres of Action Manhwa

Dungeon, Hunter & Leveling:

Protagonists awaken special classes, enter gates or dungeons, and level up using game-like status windows (e.g., Solo Leveling).

Murim (Korean Martial Arts):

Historical or fantasy settings focused on traditional martial arts sects, internal energy (qi), and rival clans (e.g., The Breaker, Memoir of the King of War).

Regression, Reincarnation & Time Travel:

A character dies or fails, then wakes up in the past with all their future knowledge to rewrite their destiny and get revenge (e.g., Reincarnation of the Suicidal Battle God).

School / Delinquent Action:

Gritty, realistic, or strategic hand-to-hand fights set in high schools and youth syndicates (e.g., Weak Hero, Lookism).

Tower / Ascension:

Characters must climb a massive, mysterious tower or go through brutal trials to reach the top and gain ultimate power or a wish (e.g., Tower of God, The God of High School).

COMEDY GENRE

The comedy genre in manhwa (South Korean digital comics) refers to stories that use humor, satire, wit, and absurd situations to entertain readers.




Core Subgenres of Action Manhwa

Action-Comedy / Dungeon Parody:

Serious power-fantasy settings where the hero or side characters have ridiculous personalities or internal monologues (e.g., Omniscient Reader’s Viewpoint or the comedic antics of monster-slaying elites).

Romantic Comedy (Rom-Com)::

Workplace or school settings driven by awkward misunderstandings, narcissistic or dorky bosses, and sparkling banter (e.g., What's Wrong with Secretary Kim).

Isekai / Reincarnation Comedy:

A normal person wakes up as a background villain, a baby, or an extra in a fantasy novel and has to scramble—often hilariously—to avoid a bad ending or just pay off debts.
